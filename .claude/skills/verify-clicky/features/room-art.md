# Room art

When the learner says "show me how this would look in my room", "on my wall", or "could I see this shirt on me" with a store page in Chrome's front tab, Clicky tries the item in a live camera panel: a wall item hangs on their wall and a standing item stands on their floor or on a table or shelf, each at its real size and kept there while the laptop turns, and clothing the page reader says can be drawn on the learner (a top, jacket, coat, vest, dress, or jumpsuit, whatever its length, sleeves, or photo) is painted on them, with nothing drawn on them before the painting.
The app reads the front tab, its largest picture, and up to 3 more product photos at least half its area, opens the panel, and calls `/try-it-item` (Claude reads where the item goes, whether it can be shown, its size, and its corners) while the camera starts.
The reading decides what follows: `/room-wall` for a wall item, `/room-floor` for a standing item on the floor or at the learner's click on a table or shelf (the local depth service measures the surface), each tracked with ECC from then on, and no depth call at all for a shirt.
While a surface is measured, only the status line shows, with no loop.
Clicky's loop traces a real shape: the learner's own top as a shirt's frame freezes and the area being painted, and the placed furniture's footprint while its painting comes, and it follows the room or the body as they move.

## Sub-features

- `room-art-route` is `/try-it-item`, which takes the main picture and up to 3 `otherPictures`, and returns the title, the item's corners in the photo (`itemCorners`, in the front picture for clothing), and an `item` that names its `kind` (`wall`, `standing`, `clothing`, or `cannotBeShown`) and holds that kind's one object. A `wall` object carries `fillsItsCorners`: true for a rectangle that fills its corners (a print, a canvas, a framed photo), false for any other outline (a round clock, a wreath, a decal).
  For a wall or standing item the object has `sizeFoundOnPage`, the size in cm, and the other sizes (a standing item also has `standsOn`, `floor` or `tableOrShelf`, and a depth), for `cannotBeShown` it has one `reason`, and for clothing it has the garment (`tshirt`, `shirt`, `sweater`, `hoodie`, `jacket`, `coat`, `vest`, `dress`, `jumpsuit`, or `other`), sleeves, `length` (where the hem ends: `waist`, `hip`, `thigh`, `knee`, or `ankle`), `canBeDrawnOnTheLearner` (the reader's verdict), size label, what the photo shows, the print, and `frontPictureIndex`, the picture that shows the garment's front.
- `room-wall-route` is `/room-wall`, which forwards to the depth service and returns the wall plane. It answers 503 without `DEPTH_SERVICE_URL`, 422 when the service finds no bare wall or the wall is nearer than 0.3 m, farther than 12 m, or under 30% on one plane, and 502 when the service is down.
- `room-floor-route` is `/room-floor`, the same body with the anchor where the furniture's front-bottom center stands, which returns the floor plane (`forward` and `floorTiltDegrees` in place of the wall's angle), with the same 503, 422, and 502 answers.
- `room-art-panel` is the camera panel: the art at its corners, the learner in front of it, and the line "Here it is at 16 by 20 inches.", with the size in inches to the nearest half inch from the measured centimeters. The status line says "Reading the page…" while the page is read.
- `room-art-furniture` is a piece of furniture, or any other object that stands on the floor such as a plush toy or a figurine, placed on the floor once the learner places its ghost (below). A page that states only the height, such as LEGO's, gets the width of the cutout's shape, and the line says "Here it is, 10 inches tall and about 5 wide." Until its painting arrives the ghost stays at half opacity and the loop traces its footprint and the status line says "Arranging it in your room…". The painting then fades in from the bare room with the line "Here it is, 31.5 inches wide and 29.5 tall.". When no painting can come, or none came within 45 s of its request going out, or 45 s have passed since the placement (even with a request still out, such as after the learner covered the box), a ready render of the store's or a generated model shows, and a painting that arrives later still crossfades over it. The could-not-draw line never comes while a painting request is out. With no request out, it comes once the model search has ended with no render and no painting can come or either 45 s wait has run out, or, after the request's own 45 s wait ran out, even with a render still coming. With no render, nothing is drawn, the loop stays whole and still with no glyph on it, and Clicky says "I couldn't draw it this time. Click the floor to try again." as a failure, and a click on the floor picks the ghost back up there to be placed again. A painting request that timed out (its own 150 s limit) is not asked for again, and a painting or a render that arrives later still shows the piece. When no floor is found and none can be inferred from the wall, "I couldn't find your floor. Tilt the screen down so the floor shows, then click the floor." with the camera still running, after which a click on the floor measures there.
- `room-art-furniture-ghost` is the true-size ghost furniture shows before anything is painted: a glowing footprint in the theme's accent with a brighter front edge, dashed corner posts, a faint top, a soft shadow, a size pill such as "90″ wide · 32″ tall", the status `Drag to move · say "right there"`, and a Place button. The theme's cursor glyph draws it line by line on first appearance, a drag moves it on the measured floor, it squares to the wall behind it within 30 cm, the scroll wheel turns it 15 degrees a step, "move it a bit to the left" nudges it (`move_furniture`), and "right there" or Place paints it for that box, facing the way the ghost faces, with the ghost at half opacity and the loop on its footprint until the painting lands. A press on the placed piece picks it back up. Clicky says "Here's where it would go. Drag it, or say right there." once as the ghost first appears, and not again after a pick-up.
- `room-art-furniture-model` is the store's own 3D model, rendered and held back as the painting's fallback, shown only when no painting can come, none came within 45 s of its request going out, or 45 s have passed since the placement, even with a request still out: found in the page (a store GLB on IKEA and Target; only a GLB is read, so a store with only a USDZ, such as Article, gets the generated model or the painted composite), rendered with the keyframe's camera and light, a top-down contact shadow, and the camera's grain, moving with the room. Each placement renders it again for that placement's keyframe.
- `room-art-furniture-composite` is the painted composite, the piece's first look about 20 s after the floor is measured, or a crossfade over the fallback when it comes later, only when the painted piece is no shorter than 85 percent of the measured box's front and no taller than 115 percent of its outline, measured down to the box's lowest corner, and not cut off along the top of the pasted region. The log line `the painted item is N% of the measured box's outline (painted rows ..., box outline ..., front face ...)` says where the painting sits against the box.
  Before the judge, the client fits a piece the painter drew too big (`furniturePaintingFit`): it shrinks the piece about the row it stands on until its top meets the box's top.
  The log then says `Room furniture: the painting's piece runs from row N to N, past what its box keeps, so it is shrunk to N% about row N`.
  A piece that floats above its footprint, stands only on a painted shadow, or would need a scale under 0.5 is not fitted, and the judge decides as before.
  A refused painting logs its rule and numbers, such as `Room furniture: the painting is refused, since it reached past the pasted region's top (100% of the top changed)`.
  Only the running Debug app, not a test run, writes each painting to the temporary folder as `clicky-room-furniture-painting-<ms>.png`.
  Its `-keyframe.jpg`, `-mask.png`, and `-box.json` sit beside it, so a refused painting can be judged again offline (`RoomFurniturePaintedComposite.itemLayers`).
- `room-art-furniture-wall` is the wall measured on the floor's keyframe from the cached depth: a floor not level with the wall is refused as a desk, and so is a plane less than 0.6 m below the camera, since a desk or a bed is level with the wall, unless the learner clicked it (the log says `the measured floor is only N m below the camera`). With no floor in view the piece stands against the wall on a floor inferred 1.05 m below the camera, and "Here it is, 31.5 inches wide and 29.5 tall." and "Tilt the screen down so I can show the whole piece." are one line when under 90 percent of the placed piece's box is in the frame.
- `room-art-page-sizes` is the page read opening collapsed Measurements, Dimensions, Product details, and Specifications sections, so IKEA's height reaches `/try-it-item`, with only size-like pairs added from the page's structured data.
- `room-art-shirt` is clothing about to be painted: once the page reads as clothing, the camera shows the learner as filmed, with no garment, outline, or loop drawn on them, Clicky says "Say paint it when you're ready, then step back and hold still. Afterward, stay in about the same pose, or I'll switch back to the still photo." once per panel (not a failure), and the status line says "Say paint it when you're ready".
  The framing's coaching ("I can't see you yet. Step in front of the camera." or "Stand back so I can see your shoulders and chest.") is never spoken, and shows in the status line only while the capture waits.
  Every garment the page reader says can be drawn on the learner takes this one path, whatever its photo, sleeves, or length.
  The log says `Room art: the garment's front is picture N of M, its cloth is #RRGGBB`.
- `room-art-shirt-painted` is the shirt's painted still: "paint it" (or its alias "right there") or the Paint it button arms a capture, Clicky says "Step back and hold still." (not a failure) and the status line says the same, or the framing's coaching while the learner is out of view or too close. The frame freezes once the learner stands in the ready framing, holds still for 0.5 s with no number shown, and then holds for a 3-2-1 countdown of 1.5 s, with no hold starting in the first 0.75 seconds after arming. The countdown shows in the middle of the panel, half a second per number, each with a soft tick ("Morse") and a different sound ("Bottle") at the freeze (no scale animation under Reduce Motion). Moving or leaving the ready framing before 3 shows starts the silent settle over, and once 3 shows only a move past 0.1 shoulder widths or leaving the ready framing stops it, so a learner holding still sees one countdown, never 3, 2, then 3, 2, 1. After 18 s with no hold Clicky says "I can't see your shoulders yet. Say paint it when you're ready." (no shake) and the panel waits for "paint it" again. "Paint it" again restarts the capture, and "go back", Back to live, a push-to-talk press, Escape, and Close cancel it. Once the frame freezes, the loop traces the area being painted with "Putting it on you…", the painted still crossfades in over 0.5 s about 20 s later with "Here it is on you." and the status "On you · keep this pose to see it live". Once the crossfade ends, the painted top follows the learner in the live camera view, and turning sideways, bending toward the camera, swaying the hips, or moving an arm far from the frozen pose fades back to the painted still with the status "Stand as you did to see it live" until the learner stands as they did; the log says `Shirt painting: the painted top follows the learner live`. "Go back" or Back to live returns to the live camera, and the ready line is not said again. The painting covers the garment's whole area: a long sleeve is painted over the bare forearm down to the wrist and a knee-length coat over the thighs, with the hands still as filmed. The log says `Shirt painting: the mask covers N% of the frame`, `painted and pasted in N s`, or `back to the live camera, because <failure>`, and the Worker logs a `[/room-furniture-composite] usage` line with `"kind":"shirt"`. The face, the hands, a watch, and the neck stay as filmed. The Worker is told the garment's kind, sleeves, length, and cloth color, which the page reader named (`mainColorHex` in the `/try-it-item` reply, and the log line `Room art: the garment's front is picture N of M, its cloth is #RRGGBB` shows it): a near-black blazer reads as a near-black color, never a mid gray, and a dark coat worn open over a white shirt reads dark, not white. A forearm that crosses the belly under a short-sleeved garment stays as filmed, and only skin under a long sleeve is painted over.
- `room-art-tracking` keeps the art on the wall, or the furniture on the floor, while the camera turns, and says "Lost the wall. Turn back, or click the wall to place it again." (or the floor's line) while the item hides.
- `room-art-loop` is Clicky's loop around a real shape: the placed furniture's footprint while its painting comes (and after it could not be drawn), and the shirt while it is painted (the outline of the learner's own top as the frame freezes, then of the area being painted, never a box over the body). It is the cursor loop's line in the theme's accent, traced and then lapped by the theme's own cursor glyph, following the room or the body, and leaving with a 0.35 s fade when the item or the shirt shows. No loop shows on a shirt before its frame freezes. While a wall, a floor, or a table is measured, no loop shows at all.
- `room-art-table-or-shelf` is a standing item with `standsOn` `tableOrShelf` (a mug, a table lamp, a vase): no floor search runs, Clicky says "Click the table or shelf where it should go." once (not a failure) with the status "Click the table or shelf where it should go", and a click measures that surface with `/room-floor` and skips the 0.6 m height check, never inferring a floor from the wall. Its lines say the surface ("Measuring the surface…", "On the table, <size>", "Lost the table. Turn back to where it stands."), and its composite request sends `standsOn: "tableOrShelf"`. A click on no flat surface says "I couldn't find a flat surface there. Click the top of a table or shelf." as a failure and keeps waiting.
- `room-art-wall-cutout` is a wall item whose outline does not fill its corners, such as IKEA's BONDIS round clock: the reading's `fillsItsCorners` is false, the log says `Room art: cut the wall item out of its photo by its own shape`, and the clock hangs round with the wall showing around it and a round shadow below it, with no white corners from its photo. A cutout Vision cannot make says "I couldn't cut it out of its photo." as a failure.
- `room-art-cannot-be-shown` are the lines for a page the reader says cannot be shown, spoken right after the page read with no camera measurement: "That isn't something I can show in your room." (`notAPhysicalItem`, a gift card), "That's too small to see in your room." (`tooSmallToSee`), "I couldn't find a clear photo of it on that page." (`noClearPhoto`), "I can only hang flat things on your wall so far." (`notFlatForTheWall`, an item that sticks out of the wall more than about 5 cm, such as a wall shelf or a sconce), and "I can show things on your wall, your floor, or a table so far." (`noPlaceClickyCanShow`, a ceiling light or curtains).
- `room-art-several-items` is a room the learner builds one item at a time: a new request while an item shows (a wall item, or a standing item whose painting or render shows) keeps the panel, the camera, the keyframe, and the tracker, and the item stays in the room as its background, drawn every frame with the learner in front of it and never picked up or moved again. The next item is measured and painted on the keyframe with the background drawn in, so "how would this lamp look on the table" and a click on the painted table's top paint the lamp standing on that table, with the table still there. A request in any other open phase closes and reopens the panel, a request whose page fails a check leaves the room as it was, and Close and Escape clear the whole room.
  A page in that room that shows nothing (an item that cannot be shown, or no size on the page) says its line as a failure and keeps the camera and the room, so the next request still places its item among the others.
  "On the table" and "on that shelf" ask for a new item only while such a room shows, and while a ghost shows the router takes those words instead.
- `room-art-kinds` are the lines for what the page cannot try: "I can't draw that item on you." right after the page read for clothing the reader says cannot be drawn on the learner (trousers, a hat, a bag), and "I couldn't find the size on that page." for a wall or standing item with no size. No garment kind, photo, sleeve length, or hem length refuses an item.
- `room-art-picture` is the picture room art hangs and sends to `/try-it-item`: the image file the page names (downloaded over https, at most 8 MB, scaled to a long side of 1280), so a cookie banner or any other element over the photo never hangs with the art, and a screen capture of the picture only when there is no file to use.
- `room-art-checks` are the lines before the camera opens: no app secret, a page that is not https, and page text that could not be read.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Open a store page for a framed print in Chrome, hold ctrl+option, and say "how would this look in my room".

## Driving it with verify-clicky.sh

Preconditions:

- The depth service runs: `cd tools/depth-service && DEPTH_PRO_CHECKPOINT=<path to depth_pro.pt> uv run server.py` (port 8801). `curl -s http://127.0.0.1:8801/health` prints `{"ok": true}`.
- The Worker from this checkout answers on the port the app calls (see `app-launch`), since it holds the two routes, and it has the `/try-it-item` route, not the older `/room-art`.
- Chrome's front tab shows a store page for wall art (not Hokusai's Great Wave; vary the item).

- **`/try-it-item`.** Build a body from a screenshot of the store page's photo, the page's own text, and the learner's words, then post it.
  Run `RUN="$(readlink .verify-clicky/current)" && screencapture -x -t jpg "$RUN/art.jpg" && sips -Z 1200 "$RUN/art.jpg" >/dev/null && python3 -I -c "import base64,json,subprocess,sys;size=dict(line.split(': ') for line in subprocess.check_output(['sips','-g','pixelWidth','-g','pixelHeight',sys.argv[1]],text=True).split(chr(10))[1:3]);print(json.dumps({'pageURL':sys.argv[2],'pageTitle':'Framed print','pageText':sys.argv[3],'learnerRequest':'how would this look in my room','picture':{'jpegBase64':base64.b64encode(open(sys.argv[1],'rb').read()).decode(),'width':int(size['  pixelWidth']),'height':int(size['  pixelHeight'])}}))" "$RUN/art.jpg" "https://shop.example/print" "Framed print, 16 x 20 in, also in 11 x 14 in" > "$RUN/try-it-item-body.json"`, then `$V post /try-it-item "$RUN/try-it-item-body.json"`.
  It answers 200 with `item.kind` `wall`, `item.wall.sizeFoundOnPage` true, and `item.wall.size` near 40.6 by 50.8 cm.
  The same body with a `pageText` that names no size answers `sizeFoundOnPage` false.
  The same request on a page for a chair, with the learner's words "how would this chair look in my room", answers `item.kind` `standing` with `standsOn` `floor` and a width, height, and depth in cm.
  A lamp page answers `standing` with `standsOn` `floor`, a rug page `standing` with a height near 1 cm, and a mug or table lamp page `standing` with `standsOn` `tableOrShelf`.
  A gift card page answers `item.kind` `cannotBeShown` with `item.cannotBeShown.reason` `notAPhysicalItem`, and the app speaks the line for that reason with no camera measurement.
- **`/room-wall`.** Build a body from a frame of a room video with the anchor at its center, then post it.
  Run `RUN="$(readlink .verify-clicky/current)" && ffmpeg -loglevel error -y -ss 1 -i <video of a room> -frames:v 1 "$RUN/wall.jpg" && python3 -I -c "import base64,json,subprocess,sys;size=dict(line.split(': ') for line in subprocess.check_output(['sips','-g','pixelWidth','-g','pixelHeight',sys.argv[1]],text=True).split(chr(10))[1:3]);print(json.dumps({'jpegBase64':base64.b64encode(open(sys.argv[1],'rb').read()).decode(),'anchor':{'u':int(size['  pixelWidth'])//2,'v':int(size['  pixelHeight'])//3}}))" "$RUN/wall.jpg" > "$RUN/room-wall-body.json"`, then `$V post /room-wall "$RUN/room-wall-body.json"`.
  It answers 200 with `distanceMeters` near the real distance in about 14 s.
  With the depth service stopped it answers 502, and the app says "I couldn't measure your room. Is the depth service running?".
- **`/room-floor`.** The same body from a frame that shows the floor, with the anchor on the floor in the lower part of the frame (`'v':int(size['  pixelHeight'])*3//4`), posted with `$V post /room-floor "$RUN/room-floor-body.json"`.
  It answers 200 with `floorTiltDegrees` under 25 and `distanceMeters` near the real distance, or 422 when no floor shows there.
- **Panel with a recorded camera.** Run `$V app-launch --room-camera-file <video of a room> "how would this look in my room"`.
  The log shows `Room art:` lines for the page read and the wall, then `Room art: N fps, track N ms, segment N ms` every 5 s.
  Run `$V shot room-art-measuring` in the first seconds, while the wall is measured: only the status line "Measuring your wall…" shows over the camera, with no loop and no outline of a bare patch of wall.
  Run `$V shot room-art` once the line "Here it is at <size>." plays: the art hangs on the wall in the panel, and a person in the video stays in front of it.
- **The panel opening.** Run the panel with a recorded camera once, and read its window's rect with a `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` read of the Clicky windows.
  The next open uses the same rect while the mouse stays on that display, since only a move or a resize saves another place.
  Run `$V app-quit` before the recording, since `app-launch` refuses to start while that Clicky still runs.
  Run `screencapture -v -V 20 -R<x>,<y>,<width>,<height> "$RUN/room-art-open.mov" & $V app-launch --room-camera-file <video of a room> "how would this look in my room"; wait`, then `ffmpeg -loglevel error -i "$RUN/room-art-open.mov" -vf fps=30 "$RUN/room-art-open-%04d.png"`.
  In the first frames that show the panel, it is faint and already fills its rect, and within about half a second it is solid.
- **The panel closing.** With the panel open and a result showing, record 4 s as above into `room-art-close.mov` and click Close during it (`swift demo/scripts/mouse/mouse.swift click <x of the panel's right edge - 32> <y of its top edge + 32>`).
  The frames show the panel fading out in about 0.15 s with its status line and buttons fading with it, and no control disappears or changes before the panel does.
  The picture and any loop hold still during the fade: no frame of the plain camera replaces a painting, a placed item, or a painted shirt.
- **Reopening during the fade.** On a wall art page with collapsed sections, such as an IKEA framed print, reload the page first so its sections are collapsed again, then run `$V app-launch --room-camera-file <video of a room> "how would this look on my wall|+40 how would this look on my wall"`.
  While the second request still reads the front tab, before the panel's status line changes, click the wall in the panel, which moves the art, so the request closes the panel and opens it again at once.
  The first request leaves the sections open, so the second read takes no 600 ms wait, and the click may come too late.
  When the log shows the second request kept the room instead, reload the page and run the step again.
  A recording shows the panel staying at full size and coming back from its fade.
  The window of time is under a second, and the unit test `aPanelOpenedAgainDuringItsCloseStaysAndFadesBackInFromWhereItIs` pins the rule.
- **Reduce Motion.** This changes a setting of the Mac, so turn it off again afterward.
  Turn on System Settings > Accessibility > Display > Reduce motion, then record the panel opening and closing as above.
  The panel fades in over about 0.15 s instead of half a second, and a click on Close counts as soon as the panel shows.
- **The status pill at the smallest size.** The panel's place is a Debug app default, so save it first with `defaults read com.crodris.clicky-watch-along roomArtPanelFrame`.
  Run `defaults write com.crodris.clicky-watch-along roomArtPanelFrame -string '{{200, 200}, {480, 270}}'`, then `$V app-launch --room-camera-file <video that turns> "how would this look on my wall"`.
  Once the video turns away, `$V shot room-art-pill-small` shows "Lost the wall. Turn back, or click the wall to place it again." whole on 2 lines in a solid dark pill, with Save photo and Close whole beside and above it.
  Write the saved value back with `defaults write com.crodris.clicky-watch-along roomArtPanelFrame -string '<the value read>'`.
  When the first read said the key does not exist, remove it with `defaults delete com.crodris.clicky-watch-along roomArtPanelFrame` instead.
- **Picture from the image file.** Leave a cookie banner up over the product photo, such as IKEA's, and run `$V app-launch --room-camera-file <video of a room> "how would this look in my room"`.
  The log says `Room art: used the page's image file (WxH) from <host>` with a long side of at most 1280, and `$V shot room-art` shows the art with no banner on it.
  A picture with no usable file (a page whose image is not https, is under 160 pixels, or is hosted on localhost or a private address) logs `Room art: ... so the picture is a screen capture` and falls back to the capture.
- **Tracking.** Launch with a video that turns, and once "Here it is" plays, take two shots a second apart.
  Run `$V app-launch --room-camera-file <video that turns> "how would this look in my room"`, then `$V shot room-art-tracking-1; sleep 1; $V shot room-art-tracking-2`.
  The art moves with the wall between the two shots, not with the frame, and the log has a `Room art: N fps` line every 5 s.
  When the video turns away from the wall, the status line says "Lost the wall. Turn back, or click the wall to place it again." until the wall returns.
- **Loop following the footprint.** Launch with a video that turns, open a furniture page, place the ghost, and take two shots a second apart while the painting comes, about 20 s.
  Run `$V app-launch --room-camera-file <video that turns> "how would this table look in my room|+40 right there"`, then `$V shot room-art-loop-1; sleep 1; $V shot room-art-loop-2`.
  The loop around the footprint moves with the room between the two shots, not with the frame.
- **Furniture on the floor.** Make a camera video from a room photo that shows the floor, such as a slow 10 s zoom with `ffmpeg -loop 1 -i room.jpg -vf "scale=1600:-2,zoompan=z='1+0.0008*on':d=300:s=1280x720:fps=30" -t 10 room-floor.mp4`, open a furniture page that states its size (IKEA's LACK side table or a POÄNG chair), and run `$V app-launch --room-camera-file room-floor.mp4 "how would this table look in my room|+40 right there"`, since nothing is painted until the ghost is placed.
  The log shows `Room art: "<title>" at <size>`, then `Room art: floor N m away, tilted N degrees`, and no wall line.
  Run `$V shot room-art-floor-measuring` while the floor is measured: only "Measuring your floor…" shows, with no loop around a patch of floor.
  When the floor arrives, the ghost is the first thing drawn: the theme's cursor glyph starts on the footprint's first corner and draws it line by line, with no loop for it to leave.
  The log line `moved the piece to where all of it shows` comes with it.
  While the painting comes, `$V shot` shows no piece, only the half-opacity ghost, the loop on its footprint, and "Arranging it in your room…".
  Run `$V shot room-art-floor` once "Here it is, <width> inches wide and <height> tall." plays: the whole piece stands on the floor, at least 4 percent inside the top and sides of the frame and clear of the status line at the bottom, near the lower middle, with its base on the floor and a soft shadow under it, and the log says `Room furniture: moved the piece to where all of it shows`.
  A piece too big for any spot stands where most of it shows, and when under 90 percent of the placed piece's box is in the frame, "Here it is" and the advice are one line: "Here it is, <width> inches wide and <height> tall. Step back so I can show the whole piece.", or "... Tilt the screen down so I can show the whole piece." on a floor inferred from the wall.
- **The ghost.** Open a furniture page that states its size (Castlery's Dawson sofa, https://www.castlery.com/us/products/dawson-sofa, or West Elm's Axel chair, https://www.westelm.com/products/axel-armchair-h2472/pip-print.html), park the mouse on the display the panel should open on, and run `$V app-launch --worker-port <port> --room-camera-file room-floor.mp4 "how would this sofa look in my room|+50 move it a bit to the left|+4 right there"`.

## Gotchas

- Depth Pro takes about 14 s warm and about 100 s for its first load, so start the service before the run. A shirt makes no depth call, so it needs no service.
- A Debug build runs the shirt's paint mask (about 6 to 8 s) and the painted top unoptimized, so both are slower than in a release build. Judge the painted top's look, not its timing, in a Debug build.
- A second Depth Pro process on the same GPU slows both. Stop any other one first.
- The depth service keeps the last two depth maps, so furniture's wall request on the floor's keyframe answers in under a second with `depthWasCached` true. A restart empties it.
- The depth service refuses a request with an `Origin` header (403) or without `content-type: application/json` (415). `$V post` goes through the Worker, which sends neither an Origin header nor a non-JSON body, but a hand-written `curl` to port 8801 needs `-H 'content-type: application/json'`.
